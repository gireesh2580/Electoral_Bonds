## 📅 Electoral Bond Scheme Timeline

### **2016**
- **May 14**: The Finance Act, 2016 amends the *Foreign Contribution Regulation Act, 2010*, allowing foreign companies with subsidiaries in India to donate to political parties.

---

### **2017**
- **January 28**: Finance Ministry seeks comments from the RBI on proposed amendments in the Finance Bill, 2017, recognizing the need to amend the RBI Act for the Electoral Bond Scheme.
- **January 30**: The RBI raises concerns about potential illicit activities, lack of transparency, and misuse of the scheme.
- **February 1**: During Union Budget 2017–18, Finance Minister Arun Jaitley announces the Electoral Bond Scheme and proposes amending the RBI Act, Income Tax Act, Representation of the People Act, and Companies Act to support it—despite RBI objections.
- **September 14**: NGO *Association for Democratic Reforms (ADR)* files a petition in the Supreme Court challenging the scheme.
- **October 3**: Supreme Court issues notices to the Centre and Election Commission in response to ADR's PIL.

---

### **2018**
- **January 2**: Central government officially notifies the Electoral Bond Scheme; donations can now be made anonymously through SBI.
- **January 7**: Finance Minister defends the scheme on social media as a step toward transparency in political funding.

---

### **2019**
- **April 12**: Supreme Court led by CJI Ranjan Gogoi directs political parties to submit details of donations, donors, and bank accounts to the Election Commission in sealed covers—but does not stay the scheme.
- **Late 2019**: RTI applications by activists like Commodore Lokesh Batra (Retd.) and Anjali Bhardwaj reveal implementation occurred despite warnings from RBI, ECI, and opposition parties.

---

### **2022**
- **November 7**: Scheme amended to increase bond sale days from 70 to 85 in election years.

---

### **2023**
- **October 16**: CJI DY Chandrachud refers scheme-related petitions to a five-judge Constitution bench.
- **October 31**: Constitution bench begins hearings on scheme challenges.
- **November 2**: Supreme Court reserves verdict on the case.

---

### **2024**
- **January**: RTI data reveals ₹571 crore in bonds sold in a 10-day window; total value over lifespan reaches ₹16,518 crore.
- **February 15**: Supreme Court unanimously strikes down the Electoral Bond Scheme as unconstitutional, citing violations of Article 19(1)(a)—freedom of speech and information. Orders SBI to submit donor-recipient data to ECI by **March 6**, and ECI to publish by **March 13**.
- **March 4**: SBI requests deadline extension until June 30.
- **March 6**: SBI misses deadline; contempt plea filed.
- **March 7**: Supreme Court receives plea for contempt action against SBI.
- **March 11**: Supreme Court rejects SBI's extension plea, orders submission by **March 12** end of business.
- **March 13**: ECI publishes bond data online, revealing major donors like:
  - Future Gaming and Hotels Pvt Ltd — ₹1,300 crore  
  - Megha Engineering  
  - Vedanta Ltd  
  Allegations arise regarding links to money laundering and corporate influence.

---

## 📌 Summary
The Electoral Bond Scheme, launched to reform political funding, faced sustained criticism over opacity and legality. Despite support from the government, regulatory institutions like the RBI and ECI raised concerns. In 2024, the Supreme Court struck down the scheme, citing constitutional violations and forced public disclosure of data.
