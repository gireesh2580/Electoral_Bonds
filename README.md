# Electoral_Bonds

#Electoral Bond Scheme Timeline
2016

May 14: The Finance Act, 2016 amends the Foreign Contribution Regulation Act, 2010, allowing foreign companies with subsidiaries in India to donate to political parties.

2017

January 28: The Finance Ministry seeks comments from the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) on proposed amendments in the Finance Bill, 2017, realizing the need to amend the RBI Act for the Electoral Bond Scheme.
January 30: The RBI expresses concerns, highlighting the scheme’s potential for illicit financial activities, lack of transparency, and possible exploitation.
February 1: The government announces the Electoral Bond Scheme during the Union Budget 2017-18, with Finance Minister Arun Jaitley proposing amendments to the RBI Act, Income Tax Act, Representation of the People Act, and Companies Act to facilitate the scheme, despite RBI’s reservations.
September 14: The NGO Association for Democratic Reforms (ADR) files a petition in the Supreme Court challenging the scheme’s legality.
October 3: The Supreme Court issues notices to the Centre and Election Commission (EC) regarding the ADR’s Public Interest Litigation (PIL).

2018

January 2: The Central government officially notifies the Electoral Bond Scheme, enabling anonymous donations to political parties through the State Bank of India (SBI).
January 7: Finance Minister Arun Jaitley defends the scheme on social media, stating it aims to create a transparent political funding system.

2019

Late 2019: Transparency activists, including Commodore Lokesh Batra (Retd.) and Anjali Bhardwaj, file RTI applications, revealing that the scheme was implemented despite warnings from the RBI, Election Commission of India (ECI), and opposition parties.
April 12: The Supreme Court, led by Chief Justice Ranjan Gogoi, directs political parties to submit details of donations, donors, and bank accounts in a sealed cover to the ECI, but refrains from staying the scheme’s implementation.

2022

November 7: The Electoral Bond Scheme is amended, increasing the sale days from 70 to 85 in years with scheduled assembly elections.

2023

October 16: A Supreme Court bench, headed by CJI DY Chandrachud, refers challenges against the scheme to a five-judge Constitution bench.
October 31: The five-judge Constitution bench begins hearings on the petitions challenging the scheme.
November 2: The Supreme Court reserves its verdict on the case.

2024

January: In the final phase before the 2024 General Election, RTI data reveals that bonds worth ₹571 crore were sold in a 10-day window, with a total of ₹16,518 crore purchased over the scheme’s lifespan.
February 15: The Supreme Court unanimously strikes down the Electoral Bond Scheme as unconstitutional, citing violations of the right to freedom of speech, expression, and information under Article 19(1)(a). The court orders SBI to disclose donor and recipient details to the ECI by March 6, with the ECI required to publish this information online by March 13.
March 4: SBI requests an extension from the Supreme Court until June 30 to disclose electoral bond details.
March 6: SBI fails to meet the court’s deadline, prompting a contempt plea against the bank for willful disobedience.
March 7: A plea is filed in the Supreme Court seeking contempt action against SBI for non-compliance with the disclosure directive.
March 11: The Supreme Court dismisses SBI’s extension plea, ordering the bank to submit details to the ECI by the close of business on March 12.
March 13: The ECI publishes the electoral bond data on its website, revealing major donors like Future Gaming and Hotels Pvt Ltd (₹1,300 crore), Megha Engineering, and Vedanta Limited, amid allegations of connections to money laundering and corporate influence.
